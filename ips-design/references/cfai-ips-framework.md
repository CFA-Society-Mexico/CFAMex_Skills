# CFAI IPS framework — what each section must contain

Distilled from the CFA curriculum (L1 Portfolio Planning and Construction; L2 Reading 47
Portfolio Management Process; L3 Reading 8 Individual Investor Portfolios; L3 Reading 32
Monitoring and Rebalancing; Standard III(C) Suitability). Use it to decide *what goes where*
and *what a grader would look for*. The deliverable is written in Spanish; section names
below give the Spanish heading to use.

## Contents
1. Why an IPS exists (use in "Propósito")
2. Process order
3. Section-by-section requirements
4. Writing rules
5. Ethics hooks (Suitability)

## 1. Why an IPS exists
- **Discipline**: objectives and limits are fixed before markets move; prevents panic selling
  and performance chasing.
- **Communication**: one shared statement of expectations between client and manager.
- **Accountability**: the benchmark against which decisions and results are judged.
- **Risk control**: constraints written down so no single decision breaks them.
- **Continuity**: a new manager can take over without disruption.
- For the client: clarity, education about their own decisions, dynamic adjustment when
  circumstances change. For the adviser: deep knowledge of the client, guidance for
  decisions, signed reference for disputes.

## 2. Process order (L3 R8)
1. Situational profile: source of wealth, measure of wealth (actual and perceived), stage of life.
2. Psychological profile: questionnaire + stated behavior; personality type.
3. Return objective: required vs desired.
4. Risk objective: ability (capacity) vs willingness; conclusion = the lower of the two.
5. Constraints: Time horizon, Taxes, Liquidity, Legal/regulatory, Unique (TTLLU).
6. Strategic asset allocation consistent with 3–5.
7. Monitoring, rebalancing, review triggers.

Mnemonic for objectives + constraints: **RRTTLLU** (Return, Risk, Time, Tax, Liquidity,
Legal, Unique). Some notes write it LLTU (L1, constraints only) or with a typo; RRTTLLU is the
complete one.

## 3. Section-by-section requirements

| # | Spanish heading | Must contain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identificación | Client name, investor type, IPS date, next review date, parties (client, adviser, other decision makers, beneficiaries) |
| 2 | Propósito y gobierno | What the portfolio is for; who decides what; approval authority; review frequency; how the IPS is amended |
| 3 | Perfil del inversionista | Source of wealth (active / passive / saved from employment) and what it suggests for risk; measure of wealth; stage of life (foundation / accumulation / maintenance / distribution); personality type with evidence |
| 4 | Objetivo de retorno | Required return with the calculation and its inputs; desired return separately; basis stated: nominal or real, pre- or after-tax, gross or net of fees; consistency with capital market expectations |
| 5 | Objetivo de riesgo | Ability with each factor and its evidence; willingness with evidence; conflict and how it was resolved; quantitative limits (max annual loss, volatility band, shortfall E(R) − 2σ when a CMA exists) |
| 6 | Restricciones | Five sub-sections, each with client evidence: Horizonte (multistage, stages named), Impuestos, Liquidez (ongoing, emergency reserve, one-time needs, illiquid holdings), Legal y regulatorio, Circunstancias únicas |
| 7 | Asignación estratégica | Approved asset classes; target and permitted range per class; role of each class; or the PM placeholder when there is no CMA |
| 8 | Lineamientos de inversión | Leverage, derivatives, short sales, concentration limits, ESG/exclusions, currency policy (hedge ratio and discretion) |
| 9 | Monitoreo, rebalanceo y revisión | Performance review frequency; benchmark; rebalancing method (calendar or corridor) and bands; review triggers; the difference between revising the IPS and rebalancing |
| 10 | Firmas | Client, adviser, date; empty lines |
| A | Anexos | Assumptions with source; open items (PENDIENTE); traceability table; glossary |

### Return objective
- Required return = what the portfolio *must* earn to fund critical goals. Non-negotiable
  given the goals; if it is unrealistic versus capital markets, say so and list the levers
  (spend less, save more, work longer, lower the bequest) instead of quietly lowering it.
- Desired return = aspirational goals. Deferrable.
- Prefer total-return language over income-vs-growth splits.

### Risk objective
- **Risk tolerance = min(ability, willingness).**
- Ability > willingness: respect willingness; note the opportunity cost and that education
  may move willingness over time.
- Willingness > ability: position by ability; educate; document it.
- Statements can mislead: cross-check stated tolerance against past behavior and holdings.

### Constraints
- Never leave one blank. "Ninguna identificada" only when the client said so; otherwise
  `[PENDIENTE: ...]`.
- Time horizon is usually multistage: name each stage, its length and what changes at the
  boundary (retirement, inheritance, sale of business).
- Taxes: state the client's facts and the rates the client or PM supplied. The adviser is
  not the tax expert; do not supply rates from memory.
- Liquidity: emergency reserve in months of expenses; one-time needs with date and amount;
  illiquid holdings (business, real estate, restricted stock) and what they imply.
- Legal: trusts, powers of attorney, contractual restrictions, fiduciary duties.
  Jurisdiction-specific regulation is a compliance item (see `revision-triggers.md`,
  "Compliance checklist").
- Unique: ESG/exclusions, concentrated positions, home, bequests, multigenerational goals,
  anything that doesn't fit elsewhere.

### Strategic asset allocation
- SAA drives most of the variation in portfolio return and risk; it is the core decision.
- Only propose numbers when a capital market expectations (CMA) file is given; the
  allocation must reach the required return within the risk limit. Without a CMA, write the
  PM placeholder and the criteria the allocation must satisfy.

## 4. Writing rules
- Specific, not generic: every statement in sections 3–6 cites a client fact or a
  calculation. "El cliente tiene un horizonte largo" is generic; "34 años hasta el retiro
  (edad actual 31, retiro a los 65) y 30 años de etapa de retiro" is specific.
- Internal consistency: objectives must fit the constraints, and the risk conclusion must
  fit the return objective.
- Time-bound facts carry their date ("cartera al 2026-10-31").
- Plain professional Spanish, short paragraphs and tables. No sales language.

## 5. Ethics hooks
- III(C) Suitability: reasonable inquiry into experience, objectives and constraints before
  any recommendation; suitability judged at the portfolio level; IPS reviewed at least
  annually and on material change.
- Unsolicited trades with material impact: discuss with client; either update the IPS with
  the client's acknowledgment or decline.
- Asset Manager Code: a written IPS per client.
